History & Architecture

The Jagannath Temple in Puri is an important Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Jagannath, a form of Vishnu. Located on the eastern coast of India, the temple was built in the 11th century by King Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva of the Eastern Ganga dynasty. The temple is one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites.

🏛️ Architectural Marvel

The temple features Kalinga architecture with the main temple covering 400,000 square feet, surrounded by a 20-foot high wall. The main structure rises to 65 meters with intricate carvings depicting scenes from Hindu mythology.

Top Sightseeing Places

Puri Beach

1km from Jagannath Temple

Golden sandy beach along the Bay of Bengal, famous for sunrise views, sea sports, and local seafood stalls. The beach hosts the annual Puri Beach Festival.

Konark Sun Temple

35km from Puri

UNESCO World Heritage site shaped as a giant stone chariot with 24 wheels, dedicated to Sun God Surya. Built in the 13th century by King Narasimhadeva I.

Chilika Lake

50km from Puri

Asia's largest brackish water lagoon, home to Irrawaddy dolphins and migratory birds. Popular for boat rides and bird watching at Nalabana Bird Sanctuary.

Gundicha Temple

3km from Jagannath Temple

The destination of the famous Rath Yatra chariot procession, where deities stay for 9 days. Built in the 16th century, considered the birthplace of Lord Jagannath's aunt.

Narendra Tank

2km from Temple

Sacred water tank where the deities are taken for a boat ride during the Chandan Yatra festival. Surrounded by small temples and bathing ghats.

Raghurajpur Village

10km from Puri

Heritage crafts village famous for Pattachitra paintings, traditional Odisha art, and Gotipua dance. Each house doubles as an art studio.

Atharnala Bridge

2km from Temple

Ancient stone bridge built in the 13th century, still in use. Features 18 arches and is a protected monument of archaeological importance.

Odisha State Museum

60km in Bhubaneswar

Extensive collection of sculptures, copper plates, rare palm leaf manuscripts, traditional weapons, and archaeological artifacts from ancient Odisha.

Complete History & Architecture of Puri Jagannath Temple

1078-1148 AD

Construction by Eastern Ganga Dynasty

King Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva initiated construction of the temple. The temple was built in the Kalinga style of architecture with intricate carvings depicting scenes from Hindu mythology.

12th Century

Completion & Consecration

The temple was completed and consecrated by King Ananga Bhima Deva. He installed the wooden deities of Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra, establishing the unique tradition of wooden idols.

16th Century

Rath Yatra Tradition Established

The famous Rath Yatra (chariot festival) gained prominence under the Gajapati kings. The tradition of pulling three massive chariots annually became a global phenomenon.

1558 AD

Afghan Invasion & Protection

When Afghan general Kalapahad attacked, the idols were secretly moved to a safe location. The temple survived but lost many of its treasures and sculptures.

1803 AD

British East India Company Era

The British took control of Odisha but respected the temple's sanctity. They appointed a temple committee to manage affairs, marking the beginning of modern administration.

Present Day

World Famous Pilgrimage

Today, the temple is one of India's most visited pilgrimage sites, with over 1 million devotees attending the annual Rath Yatra. The temple kitchen remains the world's largest, feeding thousands daily.
