About Araku Valley

Araku Valley is a picturesque hill station in the Eastern Ghats, known for its lush coffee plantations, cascading waterfalls, and vibrant tribal culture. Situated 114 km from Visakhapatnam, this valley sits at an altitude of 910–1,400 meters, offering a cool retreat year-round.

☕ Organic Coffee Hub

Home to award-winning organic Arabica coffee. The Araku coffee has gained international fame and is exported to Europe and the USA, with cooperative farming benefiting local tribal communities.

The valley is inhabited by over 36 tribal communities including the Porja, Khond, and Valmiki. Their traditions, dances, and crafts are preserved and showcased in the Tribal Museum. The scenic train journey from Vizag to Araku, with 58 tunnels and 84 bridges, is considered one of India's most beautiful rail routes.

History & Tribal Culture of Araku Valley

Ancient

Tribal Origins

Inhabited by Porja, Khond, and Valmiki tribes for millennia, with unique customs and shifting cultivation.

19th C.

British Era

Discovery of coffee potential; railway built in 1890s opening the valley.

1920s

Coffee Boom

Large-scale Arabica plantations under British supervision.

2000s

Organic Revolution

Araku cooperative formed; coffee wins international awards.

36 communities including Porja, Khond, Valmiki
