Discover Munnar

History & Tea Heritage

Munnar, meaning "three rivers" (Mudhirapuzha, Nallathanni, and Kundali), was once the summer capital of the British in South India. The transformation began in 1877 when Scottish planters introduced tea cultivation, establishing the Kannan Devan Hills as a tea-growing region.

☕ Tea Capital of Kerala

The rolling hills are carpeted with tea estates producing high-quality orthodox tea. The Tata Tea Museum (KDHP) preserves antique processing machinery and the colonial bungalow culture. Today, Munnar's tea is exported worldwide.

Top 10 Natural Attractions

Tea Gardens

Throughout Munnar

Endless rolling hills of lush green tea plantations. Walk amidst tea bushes and learn about plucking.

Eravikulam NP

15 km from Munnar

UNESCO site, home to Nilgiri Tahr. Trek to Anamudi peak from here (permit required).

Mattupetty Dam

13 km from Munnar

Scenic reservoir with boating, surrounded by hills and tea gardens.

Anamudi Peak

Inside Eravikulam NP

South India's highest peak (2,695 m). Trekking with stunning views.

Top Station

32 km from Munnar

Panoramic view of Western Ghats and Theni valley. Famous for Neelakurinji.

Echo Point

15 km from Munnar

Your voice echoes across Kundala Lake. Perfect picnic spot.

Tata Tea Museum

Nallathanni, Munnar

Antique tea processing machines, photographs and tea tasting.

Attukal Waterfalls

10 km from Munnar

Scenic waterfall amidst forest; ideal for photography.

Christ Church

Munnar Town

Historic 1910 Anglican church with stained glass and colonial charm.

Blossom Hydel Park

Near Mattupetty

Exotic flowers, boating, and well-maintained gardens.

Munnar – Ecology & Colonial Legacy

1877

First Tea Plantations

Scottish planters established tea estates; the Kannan Devan Hills became a tea hub.

1895

Kannan Devan Company

Formed to manage tea production; later acquired by Tata in 1983.

1975

Eravikulam National Park

Declared a sanctuary to protect Nilgiri Tahr; UNESCO World Heritage Site (Western Ghats).

2005

Neelakurinji Bloom

The rare flower that blooms once every 12 years turned the hills blue.
