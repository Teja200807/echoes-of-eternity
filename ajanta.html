Explore Ajanta

Ancient Buddhist Masterpiece

Ajanta Caves, carved from solid rock between the 2nd century BCE and 480 CE, consist of 29 Buddhist monuments that served as monastic retreats and worship halls. Rediscovered in 1819 by British officer John Smith, these caves represent the finest surviving examples of ancient Indian art, architecture, and painting.

🎨 UNESCO World Heritage

Recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, Ajanta showcases masterpieces of Buddhist religious art that influenced artistic developments across Asia. The frescoes depict Jataka tales, Buddha's life, and celestial beings.

The caves were created in two phases: the Hinayana period (2nd century BCE) featuring simple stupas, and the Mahayana period (5th-6th century CE) with elaborate sculptures and paintings. The site was abandoned around 650 CE when Buddhism declined in the region.

Top Cave Attractions

Cave 1

Eastern group, Ajanta

Main prayer hall with famous Bodhisattva Padmapani and Vajrapani paintings. Intricate facade and elaborate sculptures.

Cave 2

Adjacent to Cave 1, Ajanta

Monastery with ceiling paintings of geometric patterns and floral motifs. Porch with fine sculptures.

Cave 16

Central area, Ajanta

Largest Chaitya hall with inscriptions documenting donations by Varahadeva. Paintings of Nalagiri elephant taming.

Cave 17

Next to Cave 16, Ajanta

Best preserved paintings including the famous "Dying Princess" scene, and numerous Jataka tales. 30+ panels.

Cave 19

Western group, Ajanta

Ornate Chaitya hall with elaborate facade and Buddha sculptures. Horseshoe window with intricate carving.

Cave 26

Far end, Ajanta

Impressive reclining Buddha sculpture (Mahaparinirvana) and scenes of Mara's temptation. Large Chaitya.

ASI Museum

Visitor center, Ajanta

Displays artifacts, photographs, and information about cave conservation. Replicas of important paintings.

Viewpoints

Overlooking caves, Ajanta

Scenic viewpoints overlooking the horse-shoe shaped ravine and the Waghora river. Perfect for photography.

Ajanta Caves – Timeline & Artistry

2nd cent. BCE

Hinayana Phase

Early caves (9,10,12,13,15A) – simple stupas, worship halls without Buddha images.

5th‑6th cent. CE

Mahayana Phase

Vakataka dynasty patronage; ornate Chaityas, Buddha sculptures, and famous murals (Caves 1,2,16,17).

650 CE

Abandonment

Decline of Buddhism; caves hidden by jungle, preserved for centuries.

1819

Rediscovery

John Smith of the Madras Army stumbled upon Cave 10 while hunting tigers.

1983

UNESCO World Heritage

Recognised as a masterpiece of human creative genius.

Painting Technique

Fresco‑secco: mineral pigments on dry plaster, using lapis lazuli for blue.

Cave Types

9 Chaityas (prayer halls), 19 Viharas (monasteries).

Jataka Tales

Depict stories of Buddha's previous births, e.g., Sibi, Vessantara.

Patrons

Vakataka emperor Harishena, ministers Varahadeva & Buddhabhadra.
