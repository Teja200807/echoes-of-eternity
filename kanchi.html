City of Thousand Temples

Kanchipuram, known as the "City of Thousand Temples," is one of India's seven sacred cities and a major pilgrimage center. Flourishing as the capital of the Pallava dynasty between the 6th and 8th centuries, Kanchi showcases the evolution of Dravidian temple architecture across multiple dynasties.

🏛️ Architectural Evolution

Kanchi features unique Dravidian architecture spanning Pallava, Chola, and Vijayanagara periods. The Ekambareswarar Temple covers 25 acres with a 1000-pillared hall, while the Kailasanathar Temple is the city's oldest surviving structure from the 8th century.

The city is home to four Divya Desams (among 108 Vishnu temples) and three of the five Pancha Bhoota Stalams (Shiva temples representing elements). Kanchi has been a major center for Hindu philosophy, where Adi Shankara established the Advaita Vedanta school at the Kamakshi Amman Temple.

🧵 Silk Capital of India

Kanchipuram silk sarees are woven with pure mulberry silk and zari (gold thread), featuring traditional motifs like peacocks, temples, and floral patterns. Each saree takes 15-20 days to weave and can last for decades.

Top Temple Attractions

Ekambareswarar Temple

Kanchipuram (Central)

Pancha Bhoota Stalam (Earth), 25 acres, 1000-pillared hall, ancient mango tree believed to be 3500 years old.

Kailasanathar Temple

West Kanchipuram

8th century Pallava architecture, pure sandstone, earliest structural temple in South India with exquisite carvings.

Varadharaja Perumal Temple

Kanchipuram

Divya Desam, 32-acre complex with 100-pillared hall, sacred lizard sculptures, and exquisite Vishnu iconography.

Kamakshi Amman Temple

Central Kanchipuram

Important Shakti Peetha, Adi Shankara established Sri Chakra here. Unique seated posture of Goddess Kamakshi.

Kumarakottam Temple

Near Kamakshi Temple

Dedicated to Murugan, famous for grand Skanda Sashti festival. Depicts Murugan as a child (Kumaraswamy).

Ulagalandar Perumal Temple

West Kanchipuram

Divya Desam featuring 35-feet tall standing image of Vishnu as Trivikrama, measuring the universe.

Silk Weaving Centers

Throughout Kanchi

Traditional Kanchipuram silk saree weaving centers and showrooms. Watch weavers create masterpieces.

Kanchi Math

Near Kamakshi Temple

Monastery established by Adi Shankara, center of Advaita Vedanta philosophy, ancient manuscripts library.

Vaikunta Perumal Temple

Kanchipuram

8th century Pallava temple with unique three-tier shrine structure depicting historical events.

Handicraft Villages

Outskirts of Kanchi

Traditional craft villages producing bronze idols, stone carvings, and temple jewelry using centuries-old techniques.

Complete History & Architecture of Kanchi Temples

3rd Century BCE

Ancient Origins

Kanchipuram mentioned in early Sangam literature as a major city. Buddhist and Jain influences before Hindu renaissance.

6th-9th Century

Pallava Dynasty (Golden Era)

Kanchipuram becomes Pallava capital. Construction of Kailasanathar (8th cent) and Vaikunta Perumal temples. Development of Dravidian architecture with sandstone structures.

10th-13th Century

Chola Contributions

Cholas add mandapas and gopurams. Ekambareswarar temple expanded. Inscriptions detail endowments and daily rituals.

14th-16th Century

Vijayanagara Empire

Major expansion of temple complexes, addition of 1000-pillared halls, large gopurams. Patronage to silk weaving industry.

8th Century AD

Adi Shankara's Visit

Adi Shankara establishes Kanchi Math, installs Sri Chakra at Kamakshi Temple, revitalizing Advaita Vedanta.

Architectural Style

Dravidian with unique Pallava innovations: sandstone carving, lion pillars

Silk Heritage

Handwoven Kanchipuram silk sarees, GI tag, 5000+ families engaged

Inscriptions

Over 500 inscriptions from Pallavas, Cholas, Vijayanagara kings

Sacred Geography

Three Pancha Bhoota Stalams (Earth, Water, Fire) and four Divya Desams
