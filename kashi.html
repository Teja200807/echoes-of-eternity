History & Architecture

The Kashi Vishwanath Temple, located on the western bank of the holy Ganges in Varanasi, is one of the most revered Hindu temples dedicated to Lord Shiva. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, considered the holiest of Shiva temples. The temple has been destroyed and rebuilt several times throughout history.

🏛️ Architectural Marvel

The temple's three domes are covered with 800 kg of pure gold plating donated by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1839. The main sanctum houses the Jyotirlinga, 60 cm tall and 90 cm in circumference, housed in a silver altar.

Top Sightseeing Places

Dashashwamedh Ghat

500m from Kashi Vishwanath Temple

The main and most spectacular ghat on the Ganges, famous for the grand Ganga Aarti ceremony every evening with thousands of lamps and devotees.

Banaras Hindu University

5km from Temple

One of Asia's largest residential universities, featuring the magnificent New Vishwanath Temple, art gallery, and sprawling green campus.

Sarnath

10km from Varanasi

Where Buddha gave his first sermon. Features ancient stupas, archaeological museum with Ashoka Pillar, and Buddhist temples.

Ramnagar Fort

14km from Temple, across Ganges

17th-century fort with a museum displaying royal artifacts, vintage cars, and the famous Ramnagar temple complex.

Tulsi Manas Temple

3km from Temple

Modern temple dedicated to Lord Rama, built at the site where Tulsidas wrote the Ramcharitmanas. Walls inscribed with verses.

Bharat Kala Bhavan

BHU Campus, 5km

Museum with extensive collection of sculptures, paintings, textiles, and manuscripts dating from 1st century BC.

Assi Ghat

3km from Temple

Popular ghat where River Assi meets Ganges. Known for morning yoga sessions, cultural programs, and peaceful atmosphere.

Manikarnika Ghat

1km from Temple

The main cremation ghat, considered highly sacred. Hindus believe cremation here grants moksha (liberation from rebirth).

Complete History & Architecture of Kashi Vishwanath Temple

1194 AD

First Destruction

Original temple destroyed by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, founder of Delhi Sultanate. The temple was one of the largest and most magnificent in India at that time.

1585 AD

Reconstruction under Akbar

Raja Todar Mal, Akbar's finance minister, rebuilt the temple with Gujarati merchant's donations. The temple flourished during Mughal tolerance period.

1669 AD

Aurangzeb's Destruction

Emperor Aurangzeb destroyed the temple again and built Gyanvapi Mosque on its site. Parts of the old temple walls can still be seen adjacent to the mosque.

1780 AD

Current Structure

Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore built the current temple adjacent to the mosque. The structure stands as a testament to Hindu resilience and devotion.

1839 AD

Gold Plating

Maharaja Ranjit Singh of Punjab donated 800 kg of gold for plating the temple's three domes, giving it the nickname "Golden Temple" of Varanasi.

2021 AD

Kashi Vishwanath Corridor

Prime Minister Modi inaugurated the ₹339 crore corridor connecting temple directly to Ganges, enhancing pilgrimage experience for millions.
